Why Malaq exists

Technology has transformed how we live, and how violence can occur.

Digital technologies have created new opportunities for communication, education, economic participation and social connection. They can also be used to perpetrate, facilitate or intensify gender-based violence.

These experiences can threaten survivors' safety, privacy, dignity and economic security, and their ability to participate freely in digital spaces. Malaq works to ensure that technology contributes to empowerment rather than becoming a tool for abuse.

Understanding TFGBV

Know the signs

Recognising technology-facilitated gender-based violence

Digital abuse is often dismissed as "just online". It is not. Recognising it is the first step towards stopping it.

10

Ten forms of technology-facilitated violence, explained

Learn how each form works and the warning signs to look for.

Explore all ten

Online harassment

Threats, intimidation, sexual harassment and repeated unwanted contact through WhatsApp, TikTok, Instagram and other digital platforms.

Image-based abuse

Sharing or threatening to share intimate images or videos without a person’s consent.

AI-generated abuse

AI-generated or manipulated sexual or harmful content created and shared without consent to humiliate, threaten or silence someone.

Digital stalking

Using digital tools to track someone’s location, monitor their devices or secretly access their accounts.

Account takeover

Gaining unauthorised access to social media, email or mobile money accounts to control, exploit or harm someone.

Doxxing

Publishing or threatening to publish private or identifying information to intimidate someone or expose them to harm.

Sextortion and grooming

Using manipulation, coercion or threats to obtain sexual images, videos or other sexual material from women, girls or other targeted individuals.

Coercive control

Using phones, tracking devices, passwords, spyware or other technology to monitor, restrict or control an intimate partner.

Digital impersonation

Using another person’s identity, photographs or accounts to deceive, harass, exploit or cause harm.

Financial abuse

Using mobile money, banking platforms or other digital accounts to control, exploit or financially harm someone.

Why Uganda

Why this matters in Uganda

Digital technologies are increasingly part of everyday life in Uganda, and access to online spaces matters more and more for education, employment, enterprise, communication and civic participation.

Technology-facilitated violence can undermine people's ability to take part in these spaces safely and equally. Malaq works within the Ugandan context to strengthen prevention, survivor support, digital safety, evidence and institutional responses.

Our approach

Three strategic pillars

Together, these pillars form the framework for everything we do, from immediate support for survivors to long-term change in the systems around them.

01

Protect

We work to prevent and respond to technology-facilitated gender-based violence while strengthening survivor-centred pathways to safety, support and justice.

02

Empower

We equip women and girls, communities and other stakeholders with knowledge, skills and practical tools to navigate digital spaces safely and confidently.

03

Transform

We generate evidence, strengthen policies and institutions, and engage technology stakeholders to promote safer and more accountable digital environments.

What we do

Turning our pillars into action

Five programme areas make our work concrete, reaching individuals, communities, institutions and the platforms that shape digital life.

01

Digital safety and prevention

Digital safety education, awareness campaigns and practical resources that help people recognise and prevent technology-facilitated violence.

02

Survivor support and referrals

Information, guidance and referrals to trusted legal, psychosocial, health and digital-security services.

03

Research and evidence

Generating and documenting evidence on TFGBV to strengthen understanding, programming and decision-making.

04

Policy and advocacy

Working with policymakers and institutions to strengthen policies, systems and responses to technology-facilitated violence.

05

Technology and platform engagement

Engaging technology companies and digital platforms to promote safer, more accessible and more accountable digital environments.

For survivors

If you are experiencing digital abuse, you are not alone.

Technology-facilitated violence can be frightening, isolating and difficult to navigate. You do not have to deal with it alone.

Malaq can provide information on digital safety, help you understand your options and, where appropriate, connect you with trusted support services.
