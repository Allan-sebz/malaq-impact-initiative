The issue

Technology has changed how gender-based violence happens.

Technology-facilitated gender-based violence (TFGBV) is any act of gender-based violence that is committed, assisted, amplified or enabled through digital technologies, the internet or electronic devices.

It is broader than “online abuse.” Technology can be used to commit violence directly or to intensify existing forms of abuse, extending their impact from digital spaces into survivors’ homes, schools and workplaces.

Our approach

Three strategic pillars

Lasting change takes more than awareness. We work across the whole response: supporting survivors today, equipping women and girls for tomorrow, and fixing the systems that allow digital harm to continue.

01

Protect

Prevent and respond to technology-facilitated gender-based violence, provide survivor-centred support, and improve access to justice.

02

03

Advance research, policy reform, institutional change and safer technology practices to build a more accountable digital ecosystem.

Know the signs

What technology-facilitated violence can look like

Digital abuse is often dismissed as "just online". It is not. Recognising it is the first step towards stopping it.

Forms of technology facilitated violence.

Ten forms of technology-facilitated violence, explained

From online harassment to spyware and sextortion, learn how each form works and the warning signs to look for.

Explore all ten

Online harassment

Threats, intimidation, sexual harassment and repeated unwanted contact through WhatsApp, TikTok, Instagram and other digital platforms.

Image-based abuse

Sharing or threatening to share intimate images or videos without a person’s consent.

AI deepfakes

AI-generated or manipulated sexual images or videos created and shared without consent to humiliate, threaten or silence someone.

Digital stalking

Using digital tools to track someone’s location, monitor their devices or secretly access their accounts.

Account takeover

Gaining unauthorised access to social media, email or mobile money accounts to control, exploit or harm someone.

Doxxing

Publishing private information to intimidate someone or put them in danger.

Sextortion and grooming

Using manipulation, coercion or threats to obtain sexual images, videos or other sexual material from women, girls or other targeted individuals.

Coercive control

Using phones, tracking devices, passwords, spyware or other technology to monitor, restrict or control an intimate partner.
