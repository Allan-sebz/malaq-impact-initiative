From prevention to lasting change

We work across prevention, response, research and systems change, because ending technology-facilitated violence takes action at every level.

Our approach

Three strategic pillars

The pillars are the central framework of the organisation. Every programme we run contributes to at least one of them.

01

Protect

We work to prevent and respond to technology-facilitated gender-based violence while strengthening survivor-centred pathways to safety, support and justice.

02

Empower

We equip women and girls, communities and other stakeholders with knowledge, skills and practical tools to navigate digital spaces safely and confidently.

03

Transform

We generate evidence, strengthen policies and institutions, and engage technology stakeholders to promote safer and more accountable digital environments.

What we do

Five programme areas

The pillars describe what we believe. These programmes describe what we actually do.

01

Digital safety and prevention

Delivering digital safety education, awareness campaigns and practical resources to help individuals and communities recognise and prevent technology-facilitated violence.

  • Protect
  • Empower
02

Survivor support and referrals

Providing information, guidance and referrals to appropriate legal, psychosocial, health and digital-security services.

  • Protect
03

Research and evidence

Generating and documenting evidence on technology-facilitated gender-based violence to strengthen understanding, programming and decision-making.

  • Transform
04

Policy and advocacy

Working with policymakers, institutions and other stakeholders to strengthen policies, systems and responses to technology-facilitated violence.

  • Transform
05

Technology and platform engagement

Engaging technology companies and digital platforms to promote safer, more accessible and more accountable digital environments.

  • Transform
  • Empower

In practice

Survivor-centred at every step

Whether we are running a training session or supporting one person, the same principles apply: safety, dignity, confidentiality and informed consent.

  • Documenting evidence safely

    Guidance on safely documenting and storing evidence without increasing the risk of further harm.

  • Referrals to trusted services

    Connecting survivors with trusted legal, health, psychosocial and other support services.

  • Digital security help

    Practical guidance on securing accounts, devices and personal information.
