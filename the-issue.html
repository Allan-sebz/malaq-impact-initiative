What is Technology-Facilitated Gender-Based Violence?

Definition

A simple definition

Technology-facilitated gender-based violence is any act of gender-based violence that is committed, assisted, amplified, or enabled through digital technologies, the internet, or electronic devices.

It is broader than simply "online abuse." The technology can be the tool used to commit the violence, or it can make existing gender-based violence more severe.

Using technology or digital platforms to...

Harm, threaten, control, sexually exploit, intimidate, or abuse someone because of their gender.

Forms It Takes

Ten common forms of TFGBV

Threatening or sexually harassing women and girls through WhatsApp, TikTok, Instagram, and other platforms.
Distributing private intimate images or videos without consent — sometimes called "revenge porn."
Creating fake sexual images or videos of someone using AI and distributing them without consent.
Tracking someone's location, monitoring their phone activity, or accessing their accounts without permission.
Accessing someone's social media, email, or financial accounts to control or harm them.
Repeated online abuse, especially threats of sexual violence or physical harm.
Using digital banking, mobile money, or financial accounts to control or exploit someone.
Publishing someone's private information online to intimidate or endanger them.
Grooming, sextortion, or coercing girls and women into sending sexual material.
Using phones, GPS trackers, smart devices, passwords, or spyware to control a partner.

Why It Matters

The impact goes beyond the screen

TFGBV can cause lasting psychological harm, damage reputations and relationships, restrict women's and girls' participation in education, work and public life, and — in many cases — escalate into physical danger. Because it happens through everyday tools like phones and social media, it can feel inescapable and is often under-reported.
